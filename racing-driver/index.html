FROM NAPLES TO THE GRID

Marco Velardi discovered his love for speed at age four, karting on a makeshift track behind his grandfather's garage in Naples. By 14, he was European Karting Champion. By 19, he was in Formula 2.

Today, at 28, he is one of the most consistent and exciting drivers on the Formula 1 grid — known for his aggressive late-braking, surgical precision in wet conditions, and an unshakeable composure under pressure.

Number 27 isn't just a racing number. It's the age his father won his last motorcycle race. It's heritage, woven into carbon fiber.

The Man

Life as a Driver

BODY & MIND

Every day begins at 5:30 AM. Two hours of physical training — neck conditioning, core stability, reaction drills. The body of an F1 driver endures up to 6G in corners. You don't survive that without being an athlete first and a driver second.

Mental training is equally important. Visualization sessions, simulator work, and breathing exercises form the invisible foundation of every lap time.

THE WEEK BEFORE

Race preparation starts seven days out. Data analysis from previous years. Track walks on Wednesday. Simulator sessions to fine-tune setup preferences. Endless meetings with engineers about tire strategy, fuel loads, and weather models.

By Thursday, the car is being assembled in the garage. By Friday, the real work begins on track.

LIGHTS OUT

Race day is pure focus. A light breakfast. Team briefing at 9 AM. Final systems check. The grid walk — fans, cameras, controlled chaos. Then silence. Helmet on. Visor down. The world narrows to 305 kilometers of asphalt.

In the cockpit, time bends. Two hours feel like twenty minutes. Every decision happens in fractions of a second. There's nothing else like it.

RESET & RECHARGE

December through February is sacred time. Naples, family dinners, football with childhood friends. A week hiking in the Dolomites. Time to remember who Marco is beyond the helmet.

But it never fully stops. Training camps in January. The new car reveal in February. And then the fire ignites again — Melbourne, round one, a new year of war.
