About

Finding order in
high-dimensional spaces

My work sits at the intersection of applied mathematics, statistical learning, and computational physics. I develop methods that extract structure from noisy, high-dimensional data — from protein folding dynamics to financial market microstructure.

Before joining ETH, I worked at the Max Planck Institute for Dynamics and Self-Organization in Gottingen and completed my PhD at TU Munich on topological data analysis of turbulent flows.

I believe the fractal geometry behind this page reflects the core of my work: infinite complexity emerging from simple rules, patterns within patterns, structure at every scale.

Research

Current directions

My group works on fundamental and applied problems across several domains, unified by the thread of discovering latent structure in data.

T

Topological Data Analysis

Persistent homology and Mapper algorithms for shape analysis of high-dimensional datasets. Applications in molecular dynamics and neuroscience.

G

Graph Neural Networks

Message-passing architectures for learning on irregular domains. Spectral methods on manifolds. Current focus: protein interaction networks.

C

Computational Dynamics

Numerical simulation of nonlinear systems. Bifurcation theory, Lyapunov exponents, and chaos quantification in turbulent flows.

S

Statistical Learning Theory

High-dimensional regression, kernel methods, and concentration inequalities. Bridging theory and practice in sample-efficient learning.

F

Financial Microstructure

Modeling order book dynamics, market impact, and price formation using point processes and Hawkes models.

O

Optimal Transport

Wasserstein distances for distributional data. Applications in generative modeling, domain adaptation, and fairness in ML.
