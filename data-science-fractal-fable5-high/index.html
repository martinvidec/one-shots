01 / About

A statistician who fell into a strange attractor

I trained as a mathematical statistician (PhD, ETH Zürich, 2017) and spent my postdoc years embedded with climate physicists, where I learned that the hardest part of data science is rarely the model — it is quantifying what the model cannot know. My work since then sits at the intersection of uncertainty quantification, ergodic theory and modern machine learning.

The page you are reading is itself a small experiment: the background is the boundary of the Mandelbrot set, computed live on your GPU. As you scroll, the viewport descends roughly four orders of magnitude into Seahorse Valley — a reminder that the boundary between order and chaos has infinite detail at every scale, which is more or less how real-world data behaves too.

02 / Research

Current research directions

Uncertainty quantification

Conformal prediction and calibrated ensembles for simulators whose error structure is non-stationary — weather, epidemics, markets.

Learning dynamical systems

Identifying governing equations and invariant measures from short, noisy trajectories; Koopman-operator methods meeting deep learning.

Training dynamics of NNs

Treating gradient descent as a dynamical system: edge-of-stability phenomena, basin geometry, and what loss-landscape fractality implies for generalisation.

Scientific visualisation

Perceptually honest colour maps and GPU-accelerated tooling so that a figure never claims more certainty than the data supports.
